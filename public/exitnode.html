Evacuation system for emergency services

Registers evacuees by RFID or biometrics and tracks them across sites with custom Raspberry Pi IoT stations, giving responders a live view of who's where.

From a brainstorm to a working IoT network

The client was CIEM (Center for Integrated Crisis Management), who wanted new ideas for how IoT could support evacuation during a crisis. My group was chosen for the project on the strength of our earlier work on the anti-drone project. We landed on RFID and biometrics after brainstorming ways to register evacuees, letting people choose a fingerprint or facial scan instead of carrying a physical card if they'd rather not.

The point of issuing a card or scanning someone's biometrics is to log where evacuees are sent, much like package tracking at the post office: it tells responders how many people are still on-site at the evacuation point, how many have already moved on, and where those people currently are. A person's location updates whenever they scan in at an IoT station placed at a key location, such as a hospital or an emergency center set up somewhere like a hotel. The stations themselves are built from bio-friendly 3D-printed plastic, with a screen for feedback, and run on a Raspberry Pi.
